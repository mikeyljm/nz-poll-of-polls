Method

Poll of polls

True party-vote intention is modelled week by week as a random walk on the log-ratio scale, so shares always add to 100. Each poll is a noisy reading of that week's true value, shifted by the pollster's house effect (a persistent lean, plus drift between terms) and by an industry error shared by every poll in a term. Past election results enter as near-exact readings with no house effect, which is what anchors everything else. Because the current term has no result yet, its industry error comes from how large it has been in past terms: that is the main source of uncertainty, and the reason the bands are wider than a simple average's.

The model is fitted in PyMC every time a new poll appears, and again weekly so the estimate keeps moving forward between polls. Polls are collected from the Wikipedia polling page each morning; earlier polls come from the NZPolls dataset.

Seats

For each of thousands of simulated election results, seats are allocated under MMP: the 5% threshold or one-electorate exemption, Sainte-Laguë over 120 seats, and overhang. Each simulation's 64 general electorates come from the electorate model described below, run on that simulation's national vote. So ACT's or the Greens' electorate wins (and their threshold exemptions), and any overhang from a party winning more electorates than its party vote entitles it to, follow the same draw. The Māori electorates aren't covered by that model and use stated probabilities that move with each party's national vote.

Voting places and electorates

Election-day voting places are grouped by suburb or town using the Electoral Commission's results by voting place for every election since 2002. A place's lean is its party share minus the national share. Advance and special votes can't be placed on a map, so they enter the electorate projections as each electorate's gap between all votes and election-day votes.

Electorate projections add each place's lean to the national vote (a uniform swing), place each locality inside the 2026 boundaries, and then model the candidate vote: each candidate's personal vote plus the share of other parties' voters who typically split their vote toward them. Each seat assumes the last election's candidates stand again unless set otherwise.

This is repeated for thousands of draws of the national vote from the poll of polls, so every seat's chances carry the polling uncertainty. Each draw also adds local error, both a part shared by all seats and a part specific to each, sized from how wrong the model has been in past elections. Ratings: Safe 95% or more, Likely 75–95%, Leans 60–75%, Toss-up under 60%.

What this is not

This is an average of published polls and a description of past voting patterns, not a prediction of how anyone should vote. Polls measure intention at the time; they can all be wrong in the same direction.
